# Mana

**Mana **is spiritual energy. It is existence in motion. It permeates all of [Laniakea](../law/laniakea.md), the universe. It is both soul-stuff and the energy that makes things move. It is the force that animates the world and spins the sea. It is the breath of existence. Mana flows from place to place, through life, death, and rebirth. Every stone, wave, breath, or thought is Mana taking form. When something burns, grows, or fades, it is Mana transforming. Nothing is truly still. It is not merely magic, but the essence of vitality, action, and awareness itself. Mana is why anything can exist, act, or change at all.

Mana is never created or destroyed. It moves. It shifts endlessly between forms, from body to body, from life to death and into life again. This ceaseless transformation is the great turning of the Spiral. When a being dies, its Mana loosens and drifts into the vast motion of the world, gathering again in time as new form and new life. The [Spiral](../law/the-spiral.md) has no beginning and no end. It is the unbroken movement of being itself.

### The Ancient Way of the Island People

[Anúa](../organization/anúa.md) is the oldest spiritual belief of the [Nuitagata](../ethnicity/nuitagata.md), the common native peoples of **Nuitai**. It teaches that the world itself is divine and that all things, living or not, are as spirits that share in the same breath of **Mana**. It is a **non-dualistic, monistic** world view that does not differentiate the mundane natural world from the divine. In the old ways, before the arrival of the [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) missionaries and the spread of the teachings of [Shaka](../person/shaka.md), there was no Doctrine of Recurrence or moral judgment. Life and death were the rising and falling of Mana through all things, shifting from one form to another without reward or consequence. The [Akua](../law/akua.md) were honored as great spirits within this flow, neither above it nor apart from it.

What the **Nuitagata **call **Mana**, the people of the [Kame Kingdom](../organization/kame-kingdom.md) call **Ki**. The concept is nearly identical, reflecting the same monistic view of life and motion, and most scholars consider Ki to be a regional expression of the broader **Anuic **understanding of Mana.

### The Great Spirits of Nuitai

Throughout **Nuitai**, there are many pantheons of deities that embody aspects of **Mana**. These living forces are known as the **Akua**, great spirits that personify the natural world. The Akua are not separate from creation but are aspects of the natural world themselves. [Lanu](../person/lanu.md), the Akua of life and nature, is the scent of blooming flowers, the touch of wet leaves, and the gentle rain that nourishes all things. [Mauga](../person/mauga.md), the Akua of fire and change, is the eruption of volcanoes and the burning passion of the heart. [Moanaku](../person/moanaku.md), Akua of the sea and the [Underworld](../location/the-underworld.md), is entropy, both the ocean and death itself, and the secrets beneath the waves.

Followers of **Anúa **believe that all things are imbued with Mana. The Akua are simply those beings whose spirits are vast enough to embody entire forces of nature. To hear a bird's song is to hear **Lanu **singing. To light a flame is to awaken **Mauga**. To feel the ocean’s pull or death's door is to touch **Moanaku**.

### The Lost Art of the Sky People

In ancient history, the [Lanigata](../ethnicity/lanigata.md), also known as the sky people, discovered how to carve cryptic **glyphs **into stone to bind Mana within it. Through these inscriptions they could give matter the semblance of life, creating **stone golems** and animated constructs, known as [Pohaku](../species/pohaku.md). These stone golems still wander the world, relics of a forgotten age.

Many believe that the art of binding Mana in this way trapped souls within stone, preventing them from returning to the turning of the Spiral. Others claim that the spirits within the **Pohaku **are willing guardians, choosing to remain and protect what once was. The truth has been lost with the **Lanigata **themselves, who vanished four thousand years ago.

### Mana in Motion

Today, the power of **Mana **is called upon in a different way, not through crafting stone magitech but through the movement of ones own body. Though Mana is the motion within all things, most of it flows unconsciously. To channel Mana personally is not to create or extract it, but to consciously and momentarily align oneself with that current and give it form through intention. The body acts as a vessel through which Mana can resonate more clearly. The effort lies not in summoning energy from beyond, but in attuning oneself with the flow that already moves through all things.

As Mana is the source of animation and motion, to harness Mana is to move with it. Channeling is almost always performed through movement, such as martial forms, ritual dances, or rhythmic gestures. Even simple breathing exercises can align the body with the current of Mana. The more skilled the practitioner, the less physical exertion is required to draw power; mastery is the art of effortless alignment.

For most practitioners, channeling Mana enhances the body rather than reshaping the world. Martial artists use it to sharpen their reflexes, strengthen their bodies, and heighten awareness. For those who have mastered the esoteric art of magic, they can literally project Mana outward in the form of spells, shaping energy into flame, light, spark or shadow.

The most well known martial arts in Nuitai that draw Mana are [Wai’Kenpō](../technology/waikenpō.md); the defensive water based martial art developed in the [Kame Kingdom](../organization/kame-kingdom.md), and [Lelua](../technology/lelua.md); a fiery form of acrobatic developed in [The Fire Islands](../location/the-fire-islands.md) that resembles dance as much as martial arts.

Channeling Mana feels like waking up. The senses sharpen, the heart quickens, and the body hums with flowing power. The world seems brighter and more alive. Mana can be used for healing, transformation, destruction, or the summoning of spirits. Typically, the only cost is fatigue, but if a Mana user draws beyond their limits or experience they can be bedridden for days, weeks or worse, possibly even death. For those who harness the darker energies of Mana, or Mana as manifested as flame, there are even yet more risks.

### The Spiral and the Sun

[Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) cosmology introduced the **Nuitagata **to** the Spiral**, an ancient [Sarpic](../organization/sarpa.md) model of the universe describing the ordered motion of existence. In this worldview, all beings are animated by Atoma, the individual soul, carried through life, death, and rebirth by the ceaseless turning of the Spiral. This cycle was originally seen as endless and inescapable.

The philosopher hero [Shaka](../person/shaka.md) later reinterpreted this framework. Rather than accepting recurrence as an eternal certainty, he taught that Atoma may ascend beyond the Spiral through moral action and the cultivation of wisdom, reframing **Sarpic **cosmology into a path toward salvation. What had once been an infinite wheel became, in Shaka’s vision, a rising motion of transcendence expressed through illumination, symbolized by Shaka's sun mark.

When the [Teachings of Shaka](../organization/the-teachings-of-shaka.md) reached Nuitai, many Nuitagata wove these soteriological and moral scriptures into their monistic understanding of Mana without difficulty. The Spiral was seen as a **Bandari **expression of the world’s natural motion the Nuitagata already believed in, and they regarded Shaka as the radiant personification of the sunlight, an aspect of Mana, practicing devotion to him in parallel with their own **Anuic **traditions without altering their native beliefs.

Unlike the Nuitagata's unifying concept of Mana, Bandari thought distinguishes **Atoma**, individual souls, from **Kolindala**, the current of magical energy that permeates all things. In this framework, light magic arises from refining Kolindala into its purest and most orderly form, and many Bandari spellcasters attribute their radiance to Shaka’s teachings.

Sarpic religion traditionally has Devas, immense and ancient Atoma whose long passage through the Spiral has granted them mastery over Kolindala. Though less fluid and mutable than the **Akua **of Anuic belief, they embody a similar scale of cosmic being.

### Dark Currents

In some places, particularly across the** Makua Archipelago** in the south, shamans known as [Callers](../organization/the-callers.md) have learned to weave the darker energies of **Mana**. This dark energy is not evil. We all have darkness within us, but it is dangerous to harness for the inexperienced. The Callers use it to commune with spirits of the dead, who may choose to speak or aid the living, and for casting shadow spells in self-defense. These rituals carry risk, for malevolent entities sometimes impersonate the departed, and possession is always a possibility.

The Callers respect **Moanaku**, the **Akua **of the Sea and the Underworld, even if they do not pray to him, seeing death as a natural part of life. Unlike followers of Shaka, they reject the concept of the Spiral and the Doctrine of Recurrence, for if all souls are recycled, who then answers their calls? To the Callers, souls do not dissolve back into undifferentiated Mana but persist as individual beings within Moanaku’s drowned realm.

The callers also serve the living, comforting the grieving, tending the corpses of departed loved ones, performing funerary rites and, with the blessing of [Makei](../person/makei.md), guiding spirits to rest through rituals. To them, the eternal presence of death is a reminder to have gratitude for this short, fleeting life.

The Callers were among the groups in **Nuitai **who refused the Teachings of Shaka when they spread across the islands, a resistance shared by the fire dancers of the volcanic islands of the southeast. Shaka’s reinterpretation of the Spiral contradicted their conviction that the dead do not return but remain in the underworld.

Ironically, although the Callers preserve one of the oldest surviving denominations of **Anua**, their view of the soul is far closer to the **Bandari **concept of **Atoma **than to the monistic Anuic worldview held by most Nuitagata, aligning them metaphysically with the very outsiders whose influence they rejected.

Regardless of culture or belief, all magic requires movement. Mana only responds to motion. Each tradition expresses this through its own forms, such as martial arts or ritual dancing, but no one can harness the power of Mana while still.
