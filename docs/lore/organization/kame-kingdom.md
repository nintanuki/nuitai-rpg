# Kame Kingdom

To the northwest of the maelstrom, north of the Matahari Atoll and west of the treacherous seas of [Tokelau](../location/tokelau---the-north-sea.md), is the Kame Kingdom. It is an archipelago of islands marked by sacred caves, crystal clear springs and hidden grottos teeming with native life and rumored healing powers, all carefully protected by the guardians known as the Wai'Ki Wardens. This archipelago is verdant and green, the lakes and other bodies of water along the coast covered with algae. The inhabitants of the Kame Kingdom, the [Kame](../species/kame.md), are a chelonian people; walking, talking turtles far more intelligent than ordinary sea turtles. The Kame are famous for their healing arts, and natural medicine is their primary export.

 Swimming in the surrounding seas of this kingdom are colossal turtles known as [Dai-Kame](../species/dai-kame.md), including [Kyoumikame](../person/kyoumikame.md), the largest and oldest known Dai-Kame, revered as a guardian of the islands. The kingdom is ruled by the reclusive King Kamehameha, whose presence is more often spoken of in legend than seen.

Among the Kame’s cultural inventions is the game of [Kaigara](../ritual/kaigara.md), a slow and strategic shell-capturing contest that reflects the patience and wisdom of Kame society.

### The King and the Council of Springs

Though King Kamehameha, the Ōkame, is spoken of with reverence, few among the living can claim to have met him. Decisions are instead made through the "Senkai" or the Council of Springs, an assembly of senior Wai’Ki Wardens and island elders. The council chambers are part of the natural environment, within a flooded cavern, surrounding the reflection pool. There are five elders.

The elders of the Senkai can take a very long time to come to any decision. This slow, contemplative process frustrates foreign merchants who come seeking trade, for a single question of tariffs or treaties can take entire seasons to resolve. While the Senkai governs by consensus, the Ōkame always has the last word, although he rarely chooses to speak, trusting the elders’ judgment and intervening only in moments of great importance.

> The [Kame](../species/kame.md) practice their own form of Anuic beliefs known as [Wai'Ki](../organization/waiki.md), which gracefully absorbed the teachings of [Shaka](../person/shaka.md) following contact the [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) missionaries. Unlike the Nuitagata however, who adopted the more common and communal Path of the Golden Sun, the Kame were more receptive to the introspective Path of the Polished Mirror
