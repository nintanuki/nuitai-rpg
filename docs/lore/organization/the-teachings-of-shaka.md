# The Teachings of Shaka

The Teachings of Shaka form the foundation of a major faith originating in the country of [Bandar](../organization/bandar.md), west of Nuitai. The religion centers on the moral and spiritual philosophy attributed to [Shaka](../person/shaka.md), a mortal teacher whose illumination one thousand years ago revealed the nature of recurrence, defined moral conduct, and paved a path of liberation from the cycle of physical existence. This religion is a moral reinterpretation, and sometimes considered a branch, of the much older [Sarpic](../organization/sarpa.md) tradition.

### Core Beliefs

According to Shaka’s teachings, all souls, [Atoma](../law/mana.md), are continuously recycled upon death. Life and death form a single, unbroken spiral of recurrence. While recurrence was a novel concept to the Nuitagata before the arrival of the Bandari, it was already a core cosmological assumption in Bandar for centuries before Shaka merely framed the Spiral as a prison that could be escaped by following the moral path he described. By devoting oneself to the principles taught by Shaka, the believer may escape this cycle and be reborn into Shaka’s realm of light, a paradise beyond material existence.

Temples, ritual observances, and pilgrimages structure daily life. The religion provides moral guidance, communal cohesion, and a framework for understanding both life and the mysteries of death.

In Nuitai, the principal site of pilgrimage is [Kota Emas](../settlement/kota-emas.md), and it is customary for worshipers to bow in its direction during daily prayer.

### Spread and Syncretism

When the [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) first arrived in Nuitai, they settled on the golden coasts of the Matahari islands and founded the settlement of Kota Emas. From there the teachings slowly spread to the islands surrounding the [Great Maelstrom](../location/the-great-maelstrom.md) through trade and missionary activity, and were adapted by many of the island cultures of Nuitai.

The peoples of Māra Iti, the Lanuroa Archipelago, and the [Matahari Archipelago](../location/matahari-archipelago.md) integrated the new faith with their native Anuic traditions, some incorporating Shaka as an Akua Iti of light and moral order, identifying him with [Ra Lanu](../person/ra-lanu.md).

The [Kame](../species/kame.md) adopted his doctrines and integrated it with their discipline of [Wai'Ki](../organization/waiki.md), emphasizing meditation and personal mastery.

In contrast, the [Makua Archipelago](../location/the-makua-archipelago.md) and the [Fire Islands](../location/the-fire-islands.md) rejected Shaka’s teachings altogether, maintaining adherence to older [Anuic](../organization/anúa.md) and ancestral practices.

This contact produced a wide range of syncretic variations across Nuitai, collectively referred to as the Shakan Paths.

### Schools of Thought

As the religion matured in its originating country of Bandar, two principal interpretations developed: one devotional and exoteric, the other philosophical and introspective. Both claim continuity with the original [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) scriptures but differ in emphasis and method.

#### The Path of the Golden Sun

The most widespread form of the faith, often practiced by the general population of both Bandar and Nuitai. It emphasizes devotion, ritual purity, and salvation through Shaka’s grace. Followers believe that moral conduct, prayer, and pilgrimage lead the soul to be reborn into Shaka’s realm. Communal worship, purification rites, and festivals of light are central to practice.

This path is the foundation for most syncretic expressions of the religion across Nuitai, where it has blended with local beliefs and Akua worship. Soteriological and comforting in nature, it is no wonder that this version of the teachings spread like wildfire among the poor and downtrodden masses.

For the native Nuitagata of the northeast in particular, Shaka has literally ascended to the status of an Akua and joined as a newcomer to the traditional pantheon, representing the light of the sun, previously the domain of [Lanu](../person/lanu.md), the goddess of life and nature. While the Path of the Golden Sun is more common than its counterpart everywhere, it has an even stronger foothold in the eastern half of Nuitai where the native population is largest, particularly in [Māra Iti](../location/māra-iti-islands.md), the [Lanuroa Archipelago](../location/the-lanuroa-archipelago.md).

#### The Path of the Polished Mirror

A less common but influential school focused on personal cultivation and direct understanding. Adherents teach that enlightenment is achieved not through divine intervention but through inner realization of truth. The “mirror” symbolizes the mind cleared of illusion, reflecting the world as it truly is.

The Path of the Polished Mirror appeals primarily to scholars, mystics, and individuals of education or privilege. It is particularly associated with urban centers such as **Kota Emas** and with the **Kame**, who have incorporated its principles into their discipline of Wai’Ki.

This school does not deny the existence of Shaka’s realm but interprets it as a metaphor for awakening, the state in which the individual perceives the unity of all things and transcends recurrence through understanding rather than faith. Some even go as far to as claim that Shaka himself was no different than any mortal and did not ascend himself, treating the teachings more as philosophy than faith, although this strictly secular interpretation is a minority view even within the Path of the Polished Mirror itself, and traditionalists of these school accuse the secular minority of giving the Polished Mirror a heretical reputation.

The Path of the Polished Mirror is more common in the cosmopolitan western half of Nuitai, where the settled Bandari population is largest, particularly in Kota Emas. This is the school of Shaka's teachings that the Kame integrated into their practice of Wai'Ki, the shelled inhabitants of the Kame Kingdom found its patient contemplative nature more fitting to their world view and way of life.

### Contemporary Practice

Today, both paths coexist across the Maelstrom. The more common Path of the Golden Sun dominates public and rural worship all over Nuitai, while the more esoteric Path of the Polished Mirror persists in monasteries, academic circles, and the higher clergy in cosmopolitan Kota emas.

Doctrinal disputes between the two schools are rare but enduring. In history, their relationship wasn’t purely antagonistic or separate; it was fluid, overlapping, and occasionally tense. Both paths still honor Shaka and follow the same core doctrines, and it is not uncommon for adherents might move between both paths over a lifetime.
