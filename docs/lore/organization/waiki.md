# WaiKi

The religion of the [Kame](../species/kame.md) people, [Anúic](../organization/anúa.md) in nature but with an emphasis on the sanctity of water, springs, and natural flow. They believe water is the source of all life. The teachings of Wai'Ki emphasize attuning oneself to the effortless patterns of life, living in harmony with nature, moving with rather than against the currents, and cultivating a quiet understanding and acceptance of the world without judgement. This has led some to believe the [Kame](../species/kame.md) to be too complacent. Naturally, they place great importance on the protection, sanctity and reverence of the springs and grottos of their islands, which is a task the Wai’Ki Wardens, also known as the spring guardians or grotto guardians, are charged with maintaining. The Wai’Ki Wardens themselves are masters of defense and the healing arts.

Despite their recognition of the permeation of [Mana](../law/mana.md) (which they call "Ki"), the [Kame](../species/kame.md) have their own pantheon separate from the traditional [Akua](../law/akua.md) of the rest of Nuitai. There are analogs to be observed between the chelonian gods and the more common [Akua](../law/akua.md) , with some overlap. For example; they worship two separate gods of their own that represent aspects of what is considered ’s domains, the ocean mother and the father of death. But the pantheon of the [Kame Kingdom](../organization/kame-kingdom.md) is occupied by beings that look much like turtles, as one might expect, if the statues carved into the rock faces are anything to go by.

When the [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) missionaries arrived in the [Kame Kingdom](../organization/kame-kingdom.md), after they recovered from the shock of meeting giant turtle people who can talk and bow like normal people, and spread the teachings of Shaka, the [Kame](../species/kame.md) were receptive and open minded but slow to accept this new religion, but over time allowed temples to be built on their islands and the teachings to be practiced freely. Followers of the teachings of Shaka make up a significant portion of the [Kame](../species/kame.md) people, and those who do walk the path of Shaka have successfully integrated these new beliefs with the old Wai’Ki practice of reverence for the springs, bowing to Shaka in the temples and bowing to the springs in the caves. This was not a difficult transition as the practices of mindfulness, meditation and the acceptance of the spiral of death were recognizable to the [Kame](../species/kame.md), what changed was the soteriological emphasis on moral conduct, how actions in this life dictate the next, and an organizational structure that bowed in the direction of [Kota Emas](../settlement/kota-emas.md) and [Bandar](../organization/bandar.md).
