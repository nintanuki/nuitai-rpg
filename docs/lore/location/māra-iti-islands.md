# Māra Iti Islands

There is a small chain of islands to the northeast of the Great Maelstrom, stretching roughly two hundred miles across the [Calm Ring](../article/sailing.md). This chain is sometimes called the Māra Islands, named after the largest island Māra Iti, or "Little Garden", which is still relatively small. The people here have long lived in peace, with the most dangerous creatures found only in the deepest forests and mountains of the largest island.

The islands are lush and gentle, bathed in gentle rain and soft sunlight. The forests are thick with banyan*****, palm and breadfruit trees, and the air always carries the mingled scent of sea salt and fresh flowers. The beaches are pale and fine, giving way to clear shallows filled with coral and colorful darting fish. Rivers wind lazily down from misty ridges, feeding farmland and gardens heavy with fruit. Villages are built near the beaches and in the forests near the rivers, the homes resting low on the cool dry earth beneath thatched palm roofs, the walls woven from bamboo, with entryways covered with fabrics dyed with colorful patterns of indigo, turquoise and ochre. The floors are laid with lauhala mats over packed earth, covered with soft fabrics. Courtyards bloom with ferns and flowers. Shells, feathers, and small carved charms hang from the eaves, swaying in the sea breeze. Wood carved effigies of the [Akua](../law/akua.md) are found throughout the island, at the edges of fields, beside the docks, and along the forest paths.

The people of Māra Iti live simple, contented lives. Most are fisherfolk, weavers, or farmers. They grow taro and sweet potato in soft fertile soil, gather fruit from the forests, and catch fish in nets or with spears of polished wood. When the air grows hot, they rest in the shade and mend their tools. Work is not rushed here; nothing is. What the island provides is enough, and so long as the rains come and the seas stay calm, Māra Iti remains a place of quiet abundance. This is paradise in Nuitai.

Trade with other islands is rare, for ocean travel is dangerous and few venture beyond sight of their own shores. When the weather is kind, small sailboats and outrigger canoes from neighboring islands sometimes arrive over crystal clear waters to barter cloth or tools for dried fruit, herbs, and simple jewelry. Māra Iti is known for its fine woven fabrics dyed with bright natural pigments, and for its carvings of beautifully intricate patterns and figures shaped from dead coral and driftwood.

The giant guardian moth, [Pua'Lele](../person/pualele.md), makes its home on the highest mountaintops of Māra Iti. The flapping of its wings casting a gentle breeze to the villages below, the sight of it soaring in the sky reflecting colorful light on the water a common but beautiful sight in these islands. The rare priceless silk it produces reserved for the robes of the high priests and temple tapestries. Children craft effigies and play with kites in Pua'lele's image, and once a year a festival is held where floating lanterns are released into the night sky honoring the guardian of the islands.

In Māra Iti there are two religious sects. They live peacefully (and separately) despite their disagreements. One is an old spiritual order known as the [Priests of Māra](../organization/the-nature-priests-of-māra-iti.md) whose temple is the forest itself. The garden priests are part of the regular life of the people of Māra Iti, providing services and doubling as healers, midwives and teachers. Their syncretic faith, known elsewhere simply as [Māra](../organization/māra.md), is the most popular religion in present day Nuitai among the eastern Nuitagata and the garden priests can also be found in the [Lanuroa Archipelago](../location/the-lanuroa-archipelago.md) and on the Matahari Atoll. Māra Iti is also home to the rival order of these priests, the [Maki Sages](../organization/maki-sages.md), who did not accept the integration of [Shaka](../person/shaka.md) into their native Anuic beliefs. The Sages live deep in the forest of Māra Iti and are rarely seen.

Māra Iti is the home of the characters [Kailo](../person/kailo-the-wandering-chef.md), [Hina](../person/hina-the-māra-priestess.md), and [Maika](../person/maika-the-maki-sage.md).

******The reader may be interested to know that while these banyan trees are now a common and iconic feature of Māra Iti, they are not actually native to the islands themselves. They were first introduced by Bandari missionaries over a century ago.*
