# Matahari Archipelago

The sun-washed Matahari Archipelago lies directly west of [The Great Maelstrom](../location/the-great-maelstrom.md), a chain of hundreds of islands scattered across warm waters. It is the sea of the setting sun, with islands dense with colorful tropical jungles heavy with rainfall and the sound of birds singing and other wildlife, with white beaches on the coasts, and [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) carved temples and Shakan reliefs into the cliffs.

The forests are layered with towering dipterocarp trees, strangler figs, and dense palms. On coasts and river mouths grow mangroves and nipa palms. Bamboo and rattan fill the understory. Spice plants such as clove, nutmeg, and cinnamon are native or long naturalized. Orchids, pitcher plants, and ferns thrive in the humidity, while pandan (used by the Bandari to make fluffy green sponge cake) and banana give shade near villages.

A wide variety of unique flora and fauna can be found here. Colorful birds such as hornbills and parrots, reptiles ranging from skittering lizards and pythons to saltwater crocodiles. Large mammals are more common in the north; elephants, tigers and rhinos, found nowhere else in Nuitai. Wild monkeys can also be found here, not just in the jungles but congregating around the temples and the cities. In the south, marsupials are more common, as are birds-of-paradise and monitor lizards.

Two centuries ago, settlers from the sacred and mythic land of Bandar crossed the western ocean and found safe harbor here. For reasons unknown, the route that once carried them east has since returned to a state where no winds blow and colossal sea monsters roam once again. Some believe Shaka's blessing once calmed those waters to guide his people eastward and, when their journey was complete, withdrew his protection. This mystery is a source of tales for the faithful. Whatever the cause, the path home has been closed ever since, and no contact with Bandar has been possible. Now the archipelago stands as a bridge between old and new, its people born of two worlds, but belonging fully to neither. There are no Bandari alive today in Nuitai that have set foot in or seen the lands of the resplendent Bandar, it exists in legends and chronicles now only.

At the heart of the Matahari Archipelago, on the Matahari Atoll, is the wondrous golden city of [Kota Emas](../settlement/kota-emas.md). the Bandari-founded capital and center of trade, faith, and study in Nuitai’s western seas. River-canals run through the city like veins, lined with narrow boats and market stalls. Here the Nuitai-born Bandari have elected their own Luminary, believed to be the reincarnation of one of Shaka's closest disciples and a saint, his home is in the golden temple in the heart of Kota Emas. There are a few other cities but mostly the outer islands of the archipelago are quieter, home to fishing villages, terraced rice fields, and outdoor shrines to [Shaka](../person/shaka.md), the [Akua](../law/akua.md), as well as other foreign spiritual practices that came from the far west long ago.

Kota Emas is the home of the archeological society, and here also in the Matahari islands, ancient [Lanigata](../ethnicity/lanigata.md) temples overgrown with vines can be found deep in the jungles as well as sunken ruins beneath the clear waters. Floating slands are also scattered in the skies above these western seas. While these wonders are not as numerous as they are in the [The Lanuroa Archipelago](../location/the-lanuroa-archipelago.md), scholars and archeologists are still just as busy here.

There are mythic beasts here in the Matahari archipelago as well. Colorful sea monsters swim between islands, some larger than the largest Bandari ships. The sacred beast [Ringanaga](../person/ringanaga.md) flies high in the skies, weaving between the clouds and floating islands, it's focal point the goldent temple of Kota Emas, it's reminder that the stranded Bandari are bless by Shaka's light. Nagas villages can be found deep in the forests, home to snake people that make snacks of lost wanders, and among the population of the Bandari themselves cursed were-tigers known as Cindaku transform on sunny moonless days and wreak havoc.
