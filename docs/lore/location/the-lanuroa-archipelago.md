# The Lanuroa Archipelago

To the east of the great maelstrom lies the Lanuroa Archipelago, a vast chain of islands stretching over a thousand miles from the southern [Māra Iti](../location/māra-iti-islands.md) isles to the northern waters of the Fire Islands. The Lanuroa region is the most populous and culturally central of the [Nuitagata](../ethnicity/nuitagata.md) territories, the beating heart of their oceanic civilization.

The forests and reefs of these islands are dense with strange and colorful wildlife, from birds of paradise and coral fish to creatures rarely seen elsewhere. Though its ecology resembles Māra Iti’s lush tropics, Lanuroa is rougher-edged. Storms strike harder, seas run deeper, and there are greater dangers. Stone golems, [Pohaku](../species/pohaku.md), still roam the perimeters of ancient ruins in greater number and size here than anywhere else in Nuitai, and giant sea serpents are known to rise from the depths without warning, so travel in between islands is perilous. Yet the people endure, navigating these hazards with the same grace and adaptability that define Nuitagata seamanship.

Lanuroa is famed not just for its people but for what lies beneath and above it: the greatest concentration of ruins left by the vanished Sky People. Floating islands drift high overhead, and half-buried temples hum faintly with dormant glyphs. These relics attract [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) scholars and explorers, whose presence has grown since the founding of the Nuitai chapter of the [Archaeological Society](../organization/archaeological-society-of-kota-emas.md) in distant [Kota Emas](../settlement/kota-emas.md). It is the goal of the scholars of this organization to unravel the mystery behind this ancient magical technology. The arrival of these outsiders, and the curiosity and bravery they awakened among local Nuitagata, has turned Lanuroa into a crossroads of faith, science, and myth.

For all the attention the ruins draw, Lanuroa’s essence remains human. Its people are farmers, sailors, artisans, and priests much like those of Māra Iti, guided by the same devotion to both [Shaka](../person/shaka.md) and [Lanu](../person/lanu.md), and the same respect for the living forests and ocean. The difference is one of temperament: where Māra Iti is serene and introspective, Lanuroa is pragmatic and resilient. Its culture is built on coexistence with uncertainty, the understanding that the sea, the storm, and the ancient past are neighbors, not adversaries.
