# The Makua Archipelago

To the south of [maelstrom](../location/the-great-maelstrom.md) where the seas are stormy and the sky is always cloudy and grey lies the Makua Archipelago, stretching roughly one thousand miles from east to west along the [Driftbelt](../article/sailing.md). These islands are shrouded in perpetual mist and gloom, a land where the sea itself seems haunted. The coasts are lined with black sands and eerie songs of lost souls fill the opaque air. Amphibious horrors lurk in the shallow waters near the shores, and spirits float among the countless graveyards scattered across the islands.

Despite its grim beauty, the Makua Archipelago is a place of profound spirituality and mystery. Its people have long coexisted with death, accepting it as an inevitable part of life. Among the natives of the Makua Archipelago live the [Callers](../organization/the-callers.md), spirit-speaking shamans who bridge the living and the dead, and the Ki’Aku [Witch Doctors](../organization/the-witch-doctors.md), the greatest potion masters in all of Nuitai. Both groups wield [Mana](../law/mana.md) in ways both wondrous and perilous, embodying the duality of life and death that defines the Makua islands. While solmen meditation on the acceptance of death is part of the culture here, there are some that have tested the limits of magic and alchemy to try to achieve mortality, which has always ended in death. Such dangerous experimentation is not discouraged here, but to cheat death is to try to get the best of [Moanaku](../person/moanaku.md), a fool's errand if you ask anyone who grew up walking along the black sands.

The people of the Makua islands make their lives where others would not dare. Their homes are built from black wood and roofed with palm leaves oiled to resist endless rain. They fish the shallows for eel and shellfish, harvest seaweed and salt, and cultivate hardy crops in black fertile soil growing taro, yam, and a red-fleshed fruit called blood guava that grows best under cloudy skies. Bone carving and shellwork are the islanders’ art: they fashion jewelry, charms, and spirit-masks to honor their ancestors and ward off malevolent spirits. Children grow up hearing ghost stories not to frighten them, but to teach respect.

The teachings of Shaka never took root here. To the Makua, death is both final and always near. The sea is both home and tomb. While all [Akua](../law/akua.md) are prayed to here, they especially honor [Moanaku](../person/moanaku.md), god of the underworld and the seas. The depths, the dead, and hidden things. They do so with quiet reverence, not worship but acknowledgment, as one might nod to a passing storm. His name is not invoked lightly. His child, [Moanaloa](../person/moanaloa.md), prowls these waters, a colossal eel whose ghostly glowing scales drift like stars beneath the waves. It is interesting to note that Moanaloa is the only member of the guardian beasts that both protects *and *kills the inhabitants of the islands it calls home. Though it devours both monster and man, it is still revered. The islanders make offerings of black pearls and silver fish before voyages, lest they attract its hunger.

The inhabitants of the haunted isles fear few things more than the [Manogata](../species/manogata.md), shark people who rise from the deep to raid the coasts. While they are a danger no matter where one sails on the ocean, nowhere else in Nuitai but here do they actually come ashore. Their only known predator is Moanaloa. The reader may be surprised that the people here can be quite jovial, and have a strong sense of humor, if not morbid. The ancestor worshiping islanders of the Makau archipelago have learned to live beside ghosts and monsters alike. They still sing and dance around driftwood fires, and trade and commune in bustling markets under the rain, despite the gloom, because they know better than anyone that this life is fleeting and short, and should be appreciated to the fullest.

Deep within the inner jungles and cavernous cliffs of the larger islands dwell the [Pekapeka](../species/pekapeka.md), small bat-folk who glide through the mists and roost in the stalactite forests of the Makua caves. Their soft chittering songs echo through the jungles at night, . Though shy and rarely seen, they ocassionally trade fruit, shells, and glow-moss with coastal villagers, and are regarded as lucky spirits of the dark forest rather than beasts.

The Makua Archipelago is home to the characters [Tawiri](../person/tawiri-the-elderly-caller.md) and [Kiki](../person/kiki-the-witch-doctor.md).
