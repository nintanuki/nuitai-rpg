# Nuitagata

The Nuitagata are the most common native peoples of the islands of Nuitai. Broadly human in appearance, they are a large and hardy folk; tall, strong, and broad of build. It is not unusual for some of them to reach heights of eight feet tall.

Their skin ranges through shades of deep bronze, while their dense, coarse hair falls in heavy waves, ranging in hue from dark brown to fiery red. Their eyes are strikingly varied, with pupils in every color of the spectrum; heterochromia is common. The men grow their hair long but keep it tied back, and facial hair is sparse and usually shaved. It is a longstanding tradition among the Nuitagata to mark their skin with tattoos.

They are the descendants of the first inhabitants to settle the archipelago, long before the arrival of the Bandari or the rediscovery of the ruins of the [Lanigata](../ethnicity/lanigata.md). Their ancestors were unparalleled sailors and explorers. Their communities are scattered across nearly every island chain within the Calm Ring and Driftbelt, from the gentle shores of [Māra Iti](../location/māra-iti-islands.md) to the mist-shrouded [Makua Archipelago](../location/the-makua-archipelago.md) and the golden coast of Matahari.

Spiritually, the Nuitagata traditionally follow the ancestral belief known as [Anúa](../organization/anúa.md), the native faith of Nuitai. Anúa teaches that the world itself and all things within it are divine, and that everything moves because of [Mana](../law/mana.md) the spiritual energy that animates existence, and nothing truly stands still. When missionaries from the distant country of [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) made contact with these islands and introduced the teachings of [Shaka](../person/shaka.md), many Nuitagata slowly began integrating these new ideas with their native beliefs, resulting in a variety of syncretic religions practices around the islands of the [maelstrom](../location/the-great-maelstrom.md).

The Nuitagata speak [Nuitai’lo](../language/nuitailo.md), a continuum of closely related dialects descended from a common ancestral tongue. Each island group has its own accent, rhythm, and vocabulary shaped by local tradition, but communication between regions remains possible. Travelers may stumble over idioms or ceremonial phrasing, yet the shared structure of Nuitai’lo allows mutual understanding across the ocean.

Before the arrival of the Bandari, the Nuitagata had no form of writing and relied purely on oral traditions. The glyphs of the ancient Lanigata were a mystery to them. Even today most native Nuitagata still cannot read, the literate among them typically being priests of Shaka or travelers, although this is slowly changing. They have adopted the foreign Bandari writing system, which was not designed for Nuitai'lo, which is resulted in complexity and phonetic inconsistency.
