# Shakas Animal Lives

"Shaka’s Animal Lives" is a beloved [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) children’s book said to have been compiled centuries ago by the monk Tamba Uli, who sought to teach the youngest initiates about the Spiral through allegory. Each story recounts one of Shaka’s earlier incarnations, sometimes as a fox or hare, sometimes as a tree learning patience or a human rediscovering kindness. Some of the animals featured in these fables are unknown outside [Bandar](../organization/bandar.md) and have since passed into legend for Matahari-born Bandari, who will never see them in their lives.

One fable has long divided readers. In early versions, Shaka appears as a clever monkey who teaches the gods humility. The tale remains controversial, as some believe Shaka himself was a Vanara Bandari, and its telling often rekindles old debates best left resting.
