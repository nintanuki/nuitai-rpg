# Makei

Makei Moanaku, or Matei Moanatu as it is pronounced in the eastern island chains, is a psychopomp that guides the souls of the dead to the [The Underworld](../location/the-underworld.md). As an Akua Iti she is an aspect, and sometimes depicted as the daughter, of [Moanaku](../person/moanaku.md).

She is typically depicted as a young woman with a serene or content expression, with black lipstick and eyeshadow, wearing a garland of skulls and a lei of white, pink and violet plumeria flowers. Sometimes she is depicted as a ferryman holding an oar, covered in seaweed or kelp, navigating her passengers to the heart of [The Great Maelstrom](../location/the-great-maelstrom.md), while in other tales she drags the dead down below the earth.

Like her father, Makei is not malevolent, nor does she have a reputation to be particularly comforting to the dying, she simply appears before mortal's when they are knocking on death's door, without saying a word, walking on water, and helps them find their way to the afterlife. She is inevitable and eternally patient.

[The Callers](../organization/the-callers.md) of [The Makua Archipelago](../location/the-makua-archipelago.md) have a special relationship with Makei, she is their patron saint. It is not uncommon in the south, especially in the Makua islands, to find shrines dedicated to her with offerings of flowers, fruit, seashells or carved dead coral. These offerings are usually meant for the souls of lost loved ones, so they are guided to the underworld and not lost or stuck wandering Nuitai to roam, but sometimes offerings of bones are buried in secret as means to beseech Makei for more time.

In the east, [Matei Lanu](../person/lanu.md) is believed to be responsible for the natural decomposition of plants and animals, as well as the growth of fungus, an example of death being necessary for the continuation of life.
