# Priya

Pronounced with a rolled "r", Priya is an ancient Deva associated with love in its different forms. She is widely revered among [Sarpic](../organization/sarpa.md) communities, with a dedicated cult that places her at the center of their devotional life. Although devotion to Priya is not limited by gender, she has long been especially popular among women, who often keep small charms or household shrines in her honor.

Priya is depicted as a three tailed, six armed goddess whose three pairs of hands each symbolize the three expressions of love she embodies: desire, companionship, and compassion. These expressions are also treated as three distinct Devas who can act independently when invoked:

- Kamapri represents romantic excitement, passion, longing and attraction.

- Kayapri governs partnership, marriage and the steady bonds of shared life.

- Karunapri expresses kindness, generosity, compassion and care for friends, family and the larger world.

All three are considered true manifestations of Priya, each reflecting a different way that relationships shape life within the Spiral.

In artistic depictions and statues, Priya’s upper right hand holds a flaming heart while her upper left hand forms the beckoning gesture representing the passion and allure of Kamapri. Her middle pair of hands is shown clasped together or linked by a thin red thread, representing the companionship of loyalty of Kayapri. Her lower right hand holds a bowl of rice, and her lower left hand rests open on her thigh, palm upward, reflecting the generosity and openness of Karunapri. Priya is usually depicted seated and composed, eyes serenely half open, surrounded by the serpentine motifs common to Sarpic imagery.

When each of her aspects are depicted individually they have a the normal set of two arms and one tail, but donning different colored clothing and different expressions and body language representing their unique temperaments.

Kamapri is invoked by those experiencing longing or uncertainty in matters of attraction. Kayapri remains important in marriage ceremonies, even among followers of Shaka. Karunapri is called upon during acts of charity, caregiving, and reconciliation.

Together, these three aspects cover the spectrum of how people relate to one another across their lives, and Priya remains one of the most familiar and approachable Devas in daily Sarpic practice, remaining popular even among [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md) majority devoted to [Shaka](../person/shaka.md).

In addition to her six arms, Priya is also often depicted with three furry prehensile tails. This feature appears most frequently in older Vanaran influenced art, and is common in early Sarpic depictions of the Devas. The presence of these tails is sometimes cited by scholars as further evidence that Vanara Bandari were more prevalent in the ancient past.
