# Marama

Marama is the Akua Iti of the moon and the tides. An aspect of both [Lanu](../person/lanu.md) and [Moanaku](../person/moanaku.md), he dwells in Lanu’s sky but moves through Moanaku’s time, the night. He is known as Marama Lanu when seen as the moon above, and Marama Moanaku when riding the tide below.

His light is reflected from [Ra Lanu](../person/ra-lanu.md), cast upon the waters of Nuitai, and his phases marks the passing of time for sailors, farmers, and fishers alike. In his myth the shape of his eyes represent the phases of the moon, and his pupils can appear as either shining silver, deep blue or black as night.

Marama is commonly depicted as a calm young man wearing a crown of pale coral set with small pearls and a lei of seaweed. The coral symbolizes the sea’s foundation, and the pearls its quiet treasures and the shining face of the moon. He is sometimes depicted as lounging on a crescent moon.

He carries a conch shell that calls the turning of the tides. In coastal villages, people make small offerings during the new and full moons, seeking his favor for safe voyages and plentiful catches. Sailors read his face in the sky to judge the tides, the weather, and their time at sea.

Marama is patient and distant, neither warm nor cold, his attention steady but impersonal. He embodies both the beauty of stillness and slow, gentle motion. He is both the rhythm of the ocean below and the quiet watcher of the night sky above.
