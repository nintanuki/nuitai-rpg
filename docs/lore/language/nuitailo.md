# Nuitai’lo

Nuitai’lo is the language of the native [Nuitagata](../ethnicity/nuitagata.md) people. It features a consonant–vowel syllable structure, limited consonant clusters, frequent glottal stops, and a tendency toward reduplication.

Dialectal variation occurs across regions, with the most noticeable phonetic differences found between the eastern and northeastern areas, such as the [Māra Iti Islands](../location/māra-iti-islands.md) and the [The Lanuroa Archipelago](../location/the-lanuroa-archipelago.md), and the southern and southeastern regions, including [The Makua Archipelago](../location/the-makua-archipelago.md) and [The Fire Islands](../location/the-fire-islands.md) .

In Māra Iti and Lanuroa, the word for “sun,” “day,” or “light” is pronounced Ra, while in the south it becomes La. This l–r alternation is a hallmark of regional phonetic variation. In a similar way, southern dialects often replace k sounds with t sounds, a shift not found in the north. Interestingly, however, the sacred word for “gods,” Akua, remains consistent throughout Nuitai, and it is rarely rendered as Atua.

Before the arrival of the [Bandari](../ethnicity/bandari.md), Nuitai’lo lacked a written form, and all record keeping was oral. The Nuitagata later adapted the Bandari script for their language, though it does not perfectly represent its sounds. Despite this, many Nuitagata remain illiterate, reflecting broader social and historical factors rather than linguistic ones.
